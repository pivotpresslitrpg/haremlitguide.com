A familiar can be comic relief. A familiar whose curse conceals a person is a promise the story eventually has to honor. Dungeon Champions 3 by Adam Lance and Leon West puts Sadie’s situation near the center of its opening concerns, and that gives the party’s next objective an unusually personal charge.

The domestic opening is cheeky rather than solemn. Nym and Jordan emerge from a private moment into a room where the others have plainly noticed. Zuri is helping with dinner, Merielle is socializing, and Sadie demands attention with thoroughly feline determination. The joke works because the household already has a life around the couple.

## Possessiveness is not a substitute for personality

Sadie’s attachment makes the curse plot more than a technical problem to solve. Her connection to Nym matters, as does the uncomfortable gap between what she is and the form she currently occupies. Keeping those relationships in view gives the rescue objective more weight than simply introducing another striking woman.

This is also a place to be precise about the appeal. Jealousy and exaggerated possessiveness can be entertaining fantasy devices without being evidence of a healthy real-world relationship. A reader who enjoys that heightened register may find the party’s messiness compelling. Someone who wants calmer, more evenly negotiated intimacy may feel differently.

## A different balance of adventure

The manuscript’s opening notes describe this volume as lighter on intense combat than its predecessor. They also make clear that team-building mechanics are staying, with starting sheets moved into an appendix and other calculations separated where possible. Those choices help set expectations: less fighting does not mean the book has become mechanics-free.

For returning readers, the strongest reason to continue is the unfinished business among the companions. For newcomers, start earlier; the curse, loyalties, and household jokes depend on accumulated context. This is adult ensemble fantasy whose attractions include the awkward conversation after the flirting, not only the flirting itself. Whether those conversations get enough attention remains an important part of judging the series.
