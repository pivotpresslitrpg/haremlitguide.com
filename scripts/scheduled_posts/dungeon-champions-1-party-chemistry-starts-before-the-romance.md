Competence is attractive. Competence that makes the rest of the party stronger is a much better starting point for an ensemble romance than another hero who can do everything alone. Dungeon Champions by Adam Lance and Leon West gives Jordan Cash the Battle Scholar class and places other people’s development at the center of his new life.

The opening rescue of Nym is a good example of how the book introduces its women. Her enchanted cleaning brooms are holding back goblins in a magma-lit chamber. The situation is dangerous, but her magic is also specific and faintly ridiculous. Before she becomes a possible romantic partner, she is a person trying to survive with the unusual tools she has.

## Attraction needs a party identity

Jordan’s companions bring contrasting temperaments and roles: an elven warrior, a catgirl mage, a celestial healer, and a half-gorgon cook. The appeal is not merely a range of appearances. Their different abilities give them reasons to argue, cooperate, and depend on one another. That is useful groundwork for harem LitRPG chemistry.

The rescue setup still creates a challenge. Gratitude, safety, and romantic attachment can become tangled very quickly. Readers who need lengthy courtship should not assume that a long book necessarily provides a slow emotional build. The question is whether the affection feels individual, not how many pages the adventure occupies.

## Expect systems as well as sparks

Character management is an advertised part of the experience. Classes, skills, and party planning are not garnish sprinkled over a romance novel. The manuscript separates some mechanics into dedicated sections, which helps readers choose their pace, but the team-building logic remains central.

This is a useful starting point for readers who want dungeon adventure, monster-girl romance, and the pleasure of developing a whole party. For those who want the romantic plot to monopolize the page, the stat work may feel like an extremely persistent chaperone. The enthusiasm is clear; your tolerance for the bookkeeping matters just as much.
