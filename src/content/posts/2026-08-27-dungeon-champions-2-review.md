[LitRPG harem fantasy](/blog/what-is-litrpg-harem/) is a subgenre defined by game-system mechanics—leveling, character sheets, skill trees—woven into a fantasy adventure with a central male protagonist and a growing circle of female companions. It is characterized by progression loops that reward both the characters and the reader, relationship development that unfolds alongside combat, and a strong emphasis on team identity over solo heroics.

*Dungeon Champions Book 2* by Adam Lance and Leon West operates squarely inside that definition—and then quietly exceeds several of its usual limits.

---

## What Is Dungeon Champions About?

*Dungeon Champions* follows Jordan Cash, a human changeling who woke up in the Fae Wilds with no memory and a Legendary Battle Scholar class inherited from a fallen adventurer. By the end of Book 1 he had assembled a party of ex-Sisterhood members—Merielle the stoic elf Ballistic Knight, Nym the catgirl battle mage with her familiar Sadie, the celestial healer Britnayel (Britney to everyone who isn't in a formal mood), and Zuri, a half-medusa whose class is literally called *Surprise, I'm a Chef!*—plus Skullie, a snarky lich familiar rounding out the crew. Together they formed the Society of the Defiant, destroyed a corrupted artificial dungeon core, and earned hero status and a house in Boulibar Bay.

Book 2 picks up from that win and immediately makes clear the stakes are getting larger. The opening Prelude drops readers into a clandestine meeting between Queen Leah, a dragon, a demon king, and an elven figure—all conspiring around salvaged Dungeon Core data, prototypes designed to rival the Fates themselves, and a candidate on Earth they've identified as a future Warden. Jordan's party doesn't know any of this yet. That gap between what readers know and what the characters know is doing a lot of narrative work before the first character sheet even appears.

## Why the LitRPG System Actually Works Here

Good [dungeon crawl harem fiction](/blog/what-is-dungeon-crawl-harem/) lives or dies by whether its game mechanics feel meaningful or like furniture. *Dungeon Champions* earns its stat blocks.

The Battle Scholar class is the smartest design choice in the series. Jordan gains experience by *mentoring* his party, not by leading the charge himself. There's a direct mechanical penalty—reduced experience for his companions—if he intervenes too heavily in their fights. That single rule creates genuine dramatic tension every time combat breaks out: how much does he step in? When does protecting people cost them growth? It also means the romantic and adventuring dynamics are structurally linked. His relationships with Merielle, Nym, Britney, and Zuri aren't separate from the LitRPG loop—they're embedded in it.

The character sheets themselves are handled with unusual transparency. The authors address readers directly in a note at the front: differences between Book 1 and Book 2 sheets reflect updates made in response to reader feedback. That kind of community responsiveness is worth noting, and it shows in how precisely the mechanics are explained. Nym's *Wand of the Oceans* interactions, Britney's *Thorned Angel* retributive scaling, and Zuri's extradimensional cooking storage are each distinct enough that every party member feels mechanically differentiated rather than reskinned.

## The Harem Dynamic: Warmth With Real Friction

What makes the *Dungeon Champions* harem roster interesting is that the tension in Book 1—particularly around Britney, who "struggled to adapt" to the group's new situation—isn't resolved with a simple attitude adjustment. The relationships feel like they've been built over time, with each character retaining personality traits that occasionally create friction. Merielle is reliable but stoic. Nym is adorable and younger than the rest. Britney is cunning and proud. Zuri is warm, capable, and 108 years old.

That age range alone—19 to 108—gives the group a texture you don't always get in harem fantasy. These aren't interchangeable archetypes; they're people with different histories and different adjustments to make. Jordan's telepathic bond with his Tablet-holders (a Level 7 Battle Scholar ability) means he's connected to all of them in a way that's mechanically justified but also quietly intimate.

## The Fateforged Universe and Why It Matters for Readers

*Dungeon Champions* is part of the broader Fateforged shared universe, which also includes *Isekai Emperor*, *Dark City Girls*, and *Fate's Enforcer*. The authors are clear that each series is readable as a standalone, but the Prelude of Book 2 already suggests the cosmology is doing heavier lifting—the conspirators reference "the Fates," "Wardens," "the Veil," and a Veil Tear in ways that imply a connected metaphysics across the universe.

For readers who enjoy [shared universe harem fiction](/blog/what-is-shared-universe-harem/), this is genuinely exciting infrastructure. For readers who just want dungeon-diving action, the authors have been careful to keep the essential story self-contained. You won't be lost without the other series, but you may find yourself curious about them.

---

## What Sets This Apart From Similar Series

Readers who enjoy *Dungeon Crawler Carl* by Matt Dinniman will recognize the tone—sharp, self-aware, with real consequences underneath the humor. Fans of *He Who Fights With Monsters* by Jason Cheyne will find the skill-tree granularity satisfying. Where *Dungeon Champions* distinguishes itself is in the team-management layer: Jordan's class makes the party's collective growth the actual point, which pulls the emotional center of the story toward ensemble dynamics rather than solo progression. That's a meaningful difference in a genre where lone-wolf protagonists dominate.
