## What Is LitRPG Harem Fantasy?

[LitRPG harem fantasy](/blog/what-is-litrpg-harem/) is a subgenre that fuses game-system mechanics — levels, skills, class progression, character sheets — with harem romance and fantasy adventure. It is characterized by a male protagonist who grows in power alongside a cast of compelling female companions, visible stat and ability tracking, and narrative stakes driven as much by character relationships as by combat.

*Dungeon Champions* by Adam Lance & Leon West lands squarely in this space, and it brings something genuinely distinctive to the table: a hero whose class is built entirely around making everyone around him better.

## What Is *Dungeon Champions* About?

Jordan Cash wakes up on the stone floor of a dungeon with no memories, a dead adventurer beside him, and a glowing Legendary Tablet in his hand. That's your first three pages, and the book earns every beat of it.

What follows is a tight, efficient origin story. Jordan negotiates with a chaos-aligned Dungeon Core — a floating crystalline eyeball with a posh British affect and a dry sense of humor — for his survival and his class. The Core wants its dungeon cleared, a rival Demon Core destroyed, and a group of trapped low-level adventurers rescued. Jordan wants to not die in his pajamas. A deal is struck.

The class selection sequence at the end of Chapter 2 is where *Dungeon Champions* reveals its real ambition. Jordan is offered three Legendary classes — Lord of Dungeons, Core Wielder, and Battle Scholar — and the decision he makes shapes the entire premise of the series. He picks Battle Scholar, a class explicitly designed around mentoring, coordinating, and empowering a team. In a genre where protagonists usually hoard power, Jordan's class *requires* him to share it.

---

## Why the Battle Scholar Class Is a Smart Design Choice

The Battle Scholar's mechanics are worth dwelling on, because they're doing real narrative work. The class generates Epic Class Tablets that Jordan can assign to teammates, effectively granting them their classes and keeping them under his Command Authority. It rewards mentoring over solo combat — Jordan actually earns *reduced* experience from direct fighting. The telepathic bond at Level 7 sets up future team coordination in a way that should pay off romantically as well as tactically.

This is a setup that will appeal to readers who loved the party dynamics in series like *He Who Fights With Monsters* or the mentor-student tension that runs through dungeon-crawl fiction at its best. The class creates built-in reasons for Jordan to be invested in the people around him — which is exactly the structural foundation a harem narrative needs to feel earned rather than incidental.

## The Opening Hook: What the Extract Does Well

Three specific things stand out in these first chapters.

**The amnesia is handled cleanly.** Jordan wakes without memories, which is a familiar isekai device — but Lance and West use it purposefully. His retained abstract knowledge (he knows what a tablet is, has no idea where he got his dog-print pajamas) creates genuine comedy without undermining his competence. The migraine detail, landing before he even realizes he's been transported, grounds him as a character with a history before the magic begins.

**The Dungeon Core is a standout side character.** The floating eyeball negotiating terms while rolling invisible dice overhead is exactly the kind of absurd-but-internally-consistent worldbuilding that makes LitRPG fiction fun to inhabit. It's pompous, it's funny, and it's genuinely helpful without being a tutorial bot. That's harder to pull off than it looks.

**Jordan's moral compass is established early and specifically.** When the Core explains that violence is the way of this world, Jordan doesn't immediately flip into power-fantasy mode. He pushes back, asks questions, and only agrees when the Core reframes the mission as protecting goblins from a genuine threat. That's characterization, not just setup.

## A Note on the Shared Cosmology

The book's front matter flags that *Dungeon Champions* shares cosmological elements with other Adam Lance titles — *Isekai Emperor*, *Dark City Girls*, and *Fate's Enforcer* — with the explicit assurance that new readers don't need any prior knowledge. That's a generous approach to universe-building, and it adds real texture for readers already in the ecosystem without creating barriers for newcomers.

## Is *Dungeon Champions* Worth Starting Now?

This is an ongoing series, so readers who prefer completed runs may want to bookmark it and return. That said, Book 1 reads as a confident, complete opening chapter — the premise is established, the protagonist is likable and specific, and the team-building mechanics are laid out clearly enough that the direction of the series feels genuinely exciting rather than vague.

If you're the kind of reader who wants to get in early on a series with structural bones this solid, this is a good time to start.
