Soul bonds do not make a negotiation less complicated. Fate’s Enforcer 3 by Adam Lance and Anne Ominous opens with Alex Colin seated between Laura and Lana at the Golden Gate Casino, where supernatural interests are working out the consequences of a changed balance of power. Romance and public responsibility now occupy the same chairs.

The kitsune wear police uniforms and act as liaisons, not decorative escorts. Their place beside Alex reflects both an intimate connection and a professional role. That is a worthwhile development for an adult ensemble adventure: the relationships can change how the characters operate in the world, not merely where they sleep.

## The Goblin King is somebody else’s problem now

Thomas’s interest in Earth territory creates a concrete connection to Isekai Emperor. Alex must handle the local Accords, the Fates’ conditions, and other signatories’ concerns. The opening’s appeal lies in the competing obligations. Being backed by powerful beings does not eliminate the need to get other people to agree.

For readers already invested in Fateforged, that makes the crossover more substantial than a familiar name dropped into conversation. The same royal ambition that looks exciting from inside Thomas’s story becomes something an Enforcer has to administer. Different protagonists can have different reasons to regard the same development with caution.

## Read for the established circle

This is the concluding volume of Alex’s trilogy, so it should not be used as an entry point. The opening assumes existing bonds, previous conflicts, and a working familiarity with the supernatural order. A newcomer might understand the meeting while missing why its participants matter.

The scene also signals that a harem fantasy finale can spend meaningful time on negotiation. Readers wanting only combat escalation may find that less immediately satisfying; readers interested in the household’s standing and responsibilities have a stronger reason to stay.

The attraction here is an established partnership doing public work together. The glamorous clothes help. The attention to exits, authority, and competing demands gives the glamour a purpose.
